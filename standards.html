What a judge checks first

Used from Session 4

Paper Standards

Judges read many papers in a short time. Before they weigh your mathematics, they check a few plain things. A paper that fails them looks careless, however good the model. This page lists them. The Team lead checks every one before a paper is handed in.

The Rubric says how good your thinking is. This page says whether a judge can see it.

1Answer every part

  • List every task the problem sets, with its letter: (a), (b), (c) and any "generalise" or "letter" part.
  • Beside each, write the section of your paper that answers it. A task with no section is a task you have not answered.
  • Answer each task in the words of the question, so that a judge can find it.

2The summary comes first

  • One page at most, in your own words: the problem, your approach, your main results with their numbers, and what you recommend.
  • It is not an introduction. A judge who reads only the summary should know what you found.
  • Every number in the summary is the same as in the body.

3Assumptions and variables

  • Number every assumption. Give each one four parts: the assumption; its justification; where in the model it is used; and what would happen to the answer if it were wrong.
  • Every assumption is used somewhere. Delete any that are not.
  • Put every symbol in one table: the symbol, what it means, and its units.

4Charts and tables

  • Every chart and table has a number and a title: "Figure 2: Highest temperature of each year, 1990–2024".
  • Both axes are labelled, with units.
  • The source of the data is written under it.
  • The text refers to it by its number and says what it shows. A chart nobody mentions is decoration.
  • It can be read in black and white: use different line styles, not only colours.

5Numbers

  • Every number has its units.
  • Give no more figures than you can trust: 36.3 °C, not 36.3254 °C.
  • A prediction comes with a range or a "plus or minus".

6Sources and AI

  • Every piece of data and every idea that is not yours is named, with who published it and when, in a numbered list of references at the end.
  • A report on your use of AI comes from your AI Log: the tool, the job, your question and what you did with the answer.

7Length and order

  • Keep within the page limit. Long working goes in an appendix, with a sentence in the body saying what it shows.
  • Headings follow the order of the work: summary, problem restated, assumptions and variables, the model, results, testing the model, strengths and weaknesses, conclusion, references.

8The last check

The Team lead reads the summary with the body open beside it, and ticks each number, each recommendation and each chart reference. A judge will notice a mismatch faster than anything else on this page.
