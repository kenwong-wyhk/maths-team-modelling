The volleyball paper, beside the football paper

Same problem, same year, and both won. Team 2025017 chose a different sport, different countries, and a schedule that is never fixed in advance. Read it with your notes on the football paper beside you, and ask at every stop: which paper did this better? About 50 minutes. The Senior Team has more at the end.

You need the 2025 problem, the paper of Team 2025017, and your task list and notes from the football paper. Read pages 1 to 28. Pages 29 to 43 are data tables and charts; one stop asks you to look at the charts. The page numbers are the ones printed at the top of each page ("Page 5 of 43"), which match the file.

Borrow how the paper is built, never its sentences. Read it yourself. If a word stops you, use the lists below or a dictionary, not an AI summary: the point is what you notice.

What I found: open when you have done all eleven
  1. Two results: volleyball, and the 20 countries. No number of rounds, games or flight hours: those are on page 21. Most sentences name a method, usually by its initials. The football summary gave the sport, the format, the months and the sensitivity result. A director deciding whether to read on would get more from the football summary.
  2. "Team 250048", probably left over from another document: a Paper Standards check would catch it. Assumption 4 says the largest city in each country. Page 17 measures from capital cities. For the United States, Brazil and Canada these differ: New York and Washington, São Paulo and Brasília, Toronto and Ottawa. The assumption is stated, then not used.
  3. Football, with 1,318.69. It is then removed because the World Cup exists, by the rule on page 6. Volleyball and basketball have world championships too, but the rule is not applied to them. And the rule comes after the scoring: football was scored, won, and only then removed. The football paper argued the opposite: its league is new because it plays in every country. Two winning papers disagree about the World Cup, and both write their reason down. That is why a judge can accept either.
  4. Yours to argue. Words are easy to read and easy to disagree with, which is Session 3's point. AHP looks more exact, but its comparisons are judgements too, written as numbers, and the football paper never checked its matrix for consistency.
  5. After each round, every possible pairing is scored by how uncertain its result is, and the most uncertain pairings play next; travel and appeal break ties. A match everyone can predict tells you nothing new. No: each round depends on the last, so no full schedule can exist in advance. Task 2 asks for one, so the paper shows a simulated season instead (page 19). The football paper had the same problem after its group stage, and gave a sample (its page 21).
  6. Three. 6 hours here; 5 hours in the football paper, from Heid & Wu (2023); and "two or more time zones" in the part of the ChatGPT answer that this team logged. This paper says only "Research shows". Its reference list has one source on jet lag, [3] on page 27, but the text never points to it, so you cannot tell which claim it supports. To choose between them, you would have to read the sources, not the papers.
  7. 20, 18 and 20. In round 2, Cuba and Ivory Coast (CUB and CIV) do not play. The full charts on pages 35–38 show the same thing in rounds 2, 3, 5 and 7. So this simulated season has 172 games, not 180, and Cuba plays 14 matches, not 18. Page 23 says the model "balances the number of matches each team plays". The paper's own charts say otherwise, and nobody counted.
  8. Two sentences offer a reason. Page 21: "Therefore, with 9 rounds, the GSL can yield reliable team rankings", resting on 0.5121 meaning that "about 88% of match outcomes are biased toward the stronger team". Page 22: "there is no logical flaw in choosing N=9". Both come after the start of page 21, where the team tried only 8 and 9 rounds because the season lasts 8 or 9 months. The graph of entropy against rounds is a straight line, so it shows no point where more rounds stop helping: the nine comes from the calendar. The football paper counted its games from the format it chose. Both can be defended; only one is worked out.
  9. Belgium, Serbia and the Czech Republic in Europe, and the Dominican Republic in North America. Three of the four are European. The paper says it did not consider geography when choosing them, because every continent already had two teams, and it never comes back to geography when it judges the effect, which task 3b asks for. The football paper had the same result and called it a conflict with its own goals.
  10. This one: five weaknesses, each specific. The football paper gives two. Neither list has what you found by reading the whole paper, such as teams sitting out rounds, or an assumption about cities that is never used. (The list does name the largest-city assumption, but as one that might not match the real world. What you found is a different problem: the model measures from capitals instead.) That is what reading a whole paper adds.
  11. "Nov 5, 2023 version, ChatGPT-4" is word for word the example in the policy, so it was probably copied rather than checked. The jet-lag answer stops at "…", so it is not complete. The queries are numbered 1 and 3: where is 2? The football paper gave its answer in full. Honest reporting means more than saying that AI was used.
Find a kick-off time · 6 min

The home country wants the match to start between 10:00 and 22:00 (page 15). The country watching on television wants 17:00 to 26:00, that is, 2 a.m. Δt is the number of hours the watching country is ahead of the home country. Page 16 ends with the window for the start, in home time: from max(10, 17 − Δt) to min(22, 24 − Δt, 23.5 − Δt).

  1. Alone Brazil at home, Japan watching. Japan is 12 hours ahead, so Δt = +12. When can the match start, in Brazil and in Japan?
  2. Alone Japan at home, France watching. France is 7 hours behind, so Δt = −7. What goes wrong?
  3. Pair On page 16, under "Full Watch Condition", the second line has a sign the wrong way round. Which, and how can you tell?
My figures: open when you have tried all three

Brazil and Japan: from max(10, 5) = 10 to min(22, 12, 11.5) = 11.5. The match starts between 10:00 and 11:30 in Brazil, which is 22:00 to 23:30 in Japan. Japan and France: from max(10, 24) = 24 to min(22, 31, 30.5) = 22. The window starts after it ends, so no time works. Page 15 says the home team wins a conflict, but the formula does not say what to do, and the reader is left to guess. On page 16, "TB + 2 ≥ 26" should be "≤ 26": the first two hours must be over by 2 a.m., and the line after it uses ≤.

4Senior Team: go further

About 30 minutes more. A sheet helps.

  1. p. 7–8, 29 The equity score is 1 ÷ GC², and the paper never says why it squares. Using the Gini coefficients on page 29 and the popularity scores in Table 3, score each sport with 1 ÷ GC instead. Which sport wins once football is set aside?
  2. p. 11 Work out the entropy H = −p log2 p − (1 − p) log2(1 − p) for a match won with chance p = 0.5, 0.75 and 0.9.
  3. p. 18, 21, 22 Page 18 says that once two countries have played, their entropy is set to 0. With 20 teams there are 190 pairs. If every team plays every round, how many pairs have met after 8 rounds, and after 9? What is the largest the average entropy could then be? Compare with page 21. Do the same for 24 teams (276 pairs, 12 a round) after 9 and 11 rounds, and compare with page 22. What is the average really measuring?
  4. p. 19, 14 Find the USA in round 9 of Figure 9. The hatched part of its bar before the first solid block is its Arrival Plan: count those days. The formula on page 14 is AP = travel hours ÷ 24 + 1 + max(jet lag − 6, 0). Work back to the jet lag the model counted. Ignoring summer time, Washington is 17 hours behind Wellington one way round the clock, and 7 hours ahead the other way. Which did the model use, and which does the body feel?
  5. p. 13 Page 13 says flights between the countries take about 2 to 7.5 hours at a Boeing 737's cruising speed, about 840 km/h. Paris to Tokyo is about 9,700 km; Ljubljana to Berlin about 720 km. Is the range believable?
  6. p. 23–25 For baseball the best constant is d = 11. With E = 1 ÷ (1 + 10−gap ÷ d), what chance of winning does a rating gap of 11 points give? Of 50 points? The 85% accuracy was measured on the same matches used to choose d. What would you do differently?
My figures: open when you have tried all six
  1. Basketball 20.79 ÷ 0.266 ≈ 78; volleyball 3.94 ÷ 0.099 ≈ 40. Basketball wins. The choice of volleyball rests on the square, which is never explained or tested.
  2. 1, about 0.81, and about 0.47.
  3. 80 and 90 pairs. Even if every remaining pair were exactly 50 : 50, the average could be at most 110 ÷ 190 ≈ 0.58 and 100 ÷ 190 ≈ 0.53. The paper's 0.5812 and 0.5121 sit right at these ceilings. (0.5812 is a shade above 0.579, which fits the charts: some teams sit out, so fewer pairs have met.) For 24 teams: 168 ÷ 276 ≈ 0.61 after 9 rounds ("around 0.6") and 144 ÷ 276 ≈ 0.52 after 11 ("reached 0.5"). So the average mostly counts the pairs that have already played. It would fall in a straight line, as in Figure 12, whatever happened in the matches, and it cannot show that "about 88% of match outcomes are biased toward the stronger team". This is my reading of the numbers. The simulation code is listed in the appendix but not printed, so it cannot be checked directly.
  4. About 13 days, so jet lag − 6 ≈ 11 and the model counted about 17 hours: the long way round. The body adjusts by the shorter shift, 7 hours, which by the paper's own rule earns one extra day, not eleven. The USA and New Zealand spend most of round 9 resting for a jet lag that is mostly not there.
  5. No. Paris to Tokyo is about 11.6 hours; Ljubljana to Berlin under one hour. The range fits neither end, and a 737 cannot fly the long routes without stopping. The paper prints no distances, so we cannot see where the slip is. But a reader with an atlas can tell that something is wrong, and a judge might be that reader.
  6. A gap of 11 points gives 0.91; a gap of 50 gives 0.99997. With d = 11, almost any gap decides the match. Hold some matches back, choose d on the rest, and measure accuracy on the matches you held back (Session 6).
