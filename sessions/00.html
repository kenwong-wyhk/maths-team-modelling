How the Maths Team works

Gentlemen, this page records what I told you at the first meeting: what modelling is, how the teams are set up, what happens each week, and when the competitions are.

1What this team does

In mathematical modelling you use mathematics to answer a real question that nobody has answered for you. There is no answer key. Two teams can reach different answers and both be right, as long as each can say what it assumed and why that is reasonable.

A modelling competition gives every team the same real problem and about five days. Your team builds a model, tests it, and writes a paper that has to convince a judge who has never met you.

3Roles

These are the four roles of a real competition team. From Session 1 you hold one of them each week, and they rotate, so everyone holds every role.

  • Modeller. Turns the problem into mathematics, and writes down every assumption the team makes.
  • Data lead. Finds the numbers, builds the spreadsheet and draws the charts.
  • Writer. Makes the team's argument clear, on paper and when presenting.
  • Team lead. Plans the time, and makes sure the parts fit together.

Each week you move on to the next role in this order: Modeller, Data lead, Writer, Team lead, and then back to Modeller.

In a team of three, the Team lead also holds one of the other roles. Before the mock competition in Session 8, each team fixes its roles for the competitions.

Whatever your role, one job belongs to everyone: asking "why is that reasonable?" In a competition every member must understand the whole model, not only his own part.

4Routine

We meet once a week for 75 minutes, most weeks on Thursday, in the InnoCourt. I will tell you in advance when a week is different.

  • Before. About 30 minutes on your own: the task on last week's session page.
  • During. A problem, team work, the real-world answer, then a look at how an award-winning paper handled the same idea.
  • After. The session page stays online. Use it to catch up or to go further.

5Three rules

  1. No marks. You get feedback on your thinking, from me and from each other.
  2. Assumptions first. Before you calculate anything, write down what you are assuming.
  3. AI is a teammate, never the author. The first four sessions use no AI at all. After that, AI helps with named jobs such as finding data or checking language, and your team keeps a log of how it was used. AI never proposes your model and never writes your report.

7Your part

  • Be here every week. Your team cannot do your thinking for you.
  • Prepare. Do the 30 minutes between sessions.
  • Bring a charged device and a notebook.
  • Speak. Say what you think, and ask your teammates why.

8The basketball question, answered by a winning team

At the meeting your team estimated how many basketballs would fill the InnoCourt. See how a winning team would present its answer, in five screens: the question restated, the assumptions with their reasons, the model, a test of the model, and its strengths and weaknesses.

Read it for how the answer is set out, not for the number. Your team's number can be different and just as good.

9Your paper: the Hall Problem

The problem for the whole of Phase One

What is the best way to dismiss the whole school from the hall?

Every team writes one paper on this question, called its Team Paper. After an assembly every class goes back to its own classroom; you may redesign the seating plan, the exits each class uses, and the order of leaving. Each week you learn one tool on a small problem, then use it on the Hall Problem, so the paper grows every week and is complete at Session 7. The facts you are given, and a diagram of the classrooms, are on the Hall Problem page.

  1. Before Session 1 At the assembly on 25 Nov, watch the dismissal from the hall. Write down three things that slow it down, and bring them.
