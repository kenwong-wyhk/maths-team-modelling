3 | Team time

The least-squares line rises by 0.049 °C a year: about half a degree every ten years.

34.1 °C in 2036

The record is 36.6 °C. Does the model say the record will not be broken?

No. The line gives the middle of where 2036 is likely to fall. A typical year is 0.6 °C away from it, and three years out of 35 were more than 1 °C above. So: 36.3 °C, plus or minus about 0.6.

4 | What you found

Writers: what was your team's rule for the best line? Would it pick out one line only?

Today's Maths Tool: least squares. Square each gap, add the squares, and make the total as small as possible. Can you get below 12.76?

Join the first dot to the last: 36.1 at 1990, 35.6 at 2036. The line goes down. Which rule would allow that?

1990 was a very unusual year. Leave it out and the prediction rises to 36.6 °C. Are you allowed to?

4 | What you found

Part of that is 1990: one very hot year at the start flattens the line. Leave it out and the model is still 0.85 °C too cool. So is a straight line likely to be too low for 2036 as well?
