2 | Paper study

A: rated "excellent"

The summary gives the results and shows how they were found.

It looked at the residuals, not only one score of fit. Graphs, sensitivity, strengths and weaknesses all clearly labelled.

But: the simulation's rules are not stated, so nobody could repeat it.

B: rated "average"

The summary describes the context well, but has no results.

The three parts disagree. Formulas with little reason, unclear units, no sensitivity analysis.

No source cited in the text.

Our own check, against the Paper Standards: "1,922,281 e-bikes" is seven figures for a forecast. And neither summary is written to the head of the department, as the problem asked.

3 | Team time

A judge reads your summary before anything else. So does every other reader.

Team

Swap summaries with another team. Read only the summary, and write down: the plan it recommends, the number behind it, and the first place you got lost. Hand it back.

Did they write down the plan and the number you meant?

If not, the summary has failed, however good the model is.

4 | What you found

No new Maths Tool this week · today's habit

The judge's first five minutes

1 Summary

What was found, with what numbers?

2 Tasks

Is every part of the question answered?

3 One number

Find it in the body. Is it the same?

4 Charts

Titled, labelled, with a source?

5 References

Cited in the text, or only listed?

A paper that fails here is read with suspicion from then on. The Team lead does these five checks before every hand-in.

4 | What you found

A judge reads the summary. The Principal reads the letter. Neither reads your model first.

"Dear Principal, We recommend … At present the hall takes … With our plan … We would like to try it at …, and we would measure …"

Team

Start the letter: what you recommend, the number that shows it is better, what it changes for students and teachers, and how one assembly could test it. One page at most.
