Session 9

Mock Competition: defence and debrief

Two judges have read your paper. Today you defend it, find out what they saw, and decide the two things you will do differently in the real competition.

The judges' words are feedback, not a verdict on you. Every cover sheet has things the judges liked and things that could be better, because every paper has both, winning papers included.

1Before this session

About 45 minutes between the hand-in and Thursday, in two parts.

  1. Alone · 20 min, by Wednesday evening A first read of another team's paper, as a judge does it. I give you the paper and a slip. Read the summary only, and write what the paper recommends and the number behind it. Then look through the body for ten minutes, and write the one task you think it missed, and whether you would read on, with your reason.
  2. Team · 25 min Slides for eight minutes. They may show only what your paper says: nothing new, nothing fixed. Open with the question and your answer, with its number, so that the audience knows your result before your second slide. Everyone speaks, and everyone can answer about every slide.

2Present and defend

Each team presents for eight minutes. Then a teacher from outside the Maths Team asks questions for five, and chooses who answers. Expect the questions a judge asks: why that assumption, which input would change your answer, what you left out, and how you know the number is right. You may be asked about a part a teammate built.

3Every task a judge checks

  1. Team · 3 min Before I show the full list: how many tasks did the problem set? Count them from the task map you made in the first hour.
  2. Team · 4 min Then, against the full list: which tasks does your paper answer, and where? Which did your task map miss, and which did it have that the paper then dropped?

4The cover sheets

I hand each team its designation, the judges' cover sheet and the first-read slips your classmates wrote.

  1. Alone · 4 min Read them in silence.
  2. Team · 3 min Write one question you would ask the judges. Compare what the first readers took from your summary with what you meant it to say. If they wrote a different number, your summary failed its test.

5Paper study: two winning teams' slides

The teams behind the football paper (2025006) and the volleyball paper (2025017) also presented their work. Each used 41 slides. On the screen: slides 2 and 40 of the volleyball deck, and slides 5 and 40 of the football deck.

  1. Team · 4 min Which deck tells you what the team was asked to do, before it tells you what it did? Does either conclusion slide give a number?
What the slides show: open only when your team has decided

The volleyball deck lists the problem's four tasks on slide 2, and from then on a bar at the foot of each slide shows which part of the talk you are in. The football deck's slide 5 maps its own model, three parts with a method for each, but the deck never lists the tasks the team was set.

Neither conclusion gives a number. The volleyball team lists its strengths and weaknesses as labels; the football team says its model "is capable of providing an accurate schedule". Their results are in the middle of the decks, on slides that an audience that has stopped listening will not see. Compare your own first and last slides.

6Two fixes

  1. Team · 5 min Write the two things your team will do differently in the competition. Each fix is something you will do, at a time you can name: "The summary is started at hour 60", not "write a better summary".

7Before the competition

  • Your pre-competition checklist. One page, from the Paper Standards and your two fixes. The Team lead keeps it and reads it on the first day of every competition.
  • The guide to the volleyball paper, read beside the football paper.
  • If your cover sheet criticises your summary, rewrite the summary only, in about 30 minutes, and send me both versions. Which one would get your paper read in full?
